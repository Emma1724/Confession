Wife (26f) slept with 20 guys

Published by Anonymous | Marriage Confession

It was late at night, and my wife and I were lying in bed talking about the kind of random things couples talk about when neither person is ready to sleep. Nothing felt unusual. We were comfortable, relaxed, and completely ourselves around each other. Then, almost without thinking, I asked her how many people she'd been with before me. I knew she'd had a life before our relationship, obviously. She's 26, I'm 36, and neither of us came into this marriage with a blank slate. My own number is around 15, so I wasn't exactly inexperienced myself. Still, I'd never really asked her to put a number on her past.

She looked at me and said something that immediately caught me off guard. She told me she'd never actually counted, but if she had to guess, it was probably somewhere around 15 to 20. She said it almost casually, but the number landed differently in my head. Fifteen wouldn't have surprised me. Twenty sounded different. Suddenly I was staring at the ceiling, doing mental calculations I hadn't expected to be doing that night. I started wondering which number was closer to the truth. Was it really 15? Was it closer to 20? Was she simply giving me a rough estimate because she genuinely didn't remember? Or was there something she didn't want to tell me?

The strange thing was that I didn't feel just one emotion. I felt several at once. There was jealousy, definitely. But there was also curiosity, insecurity, and, surprisingly, attraction. My wife knows that I have a fantasy involving her being with other men, so hearing about her past created an uncomfortable contradiction inside me. The same information that made part of me jealous also fascinated another part of me. I found myself wanting to know more while simultaneously wondering whether knowing more would only make things harder.

I started asking questions, trying to understand what her relationships had been like before me. She told me about different people she'd dated and experiences she'd had before our relationship. Some stories were serious, others were casual, and some apparently didn't mean very much to her at all. One particular detail about an uncomfortable experience with someone from her past stuck in my mind, not because I wanted to know every detail, but because it reminded me just how much of her life existed before I ever came along. There were people who knew her before I did. There were memories I wasn't part of. There were entire chapters of her life that I'd never witnessed.

That realization hit me harder than the number itself. I suddenly started thinking about what it meant to meet someone who already had years of experiences, relationships, mistakes, memories, and people behind them. When I met her, I was meeting the person she had become after all of those experiences. But I hadn't been there to see how she became that person. I only knew the version of her who eventually became my wife.

At one point, the conversation turned toward whether she'd ever received particularly strong compliments from people she'd been with. She said she didn't remember many people saying things like that, although she did remember one person telling her that he particularly enjoyed being with her. Again, my reaction surprised me. I felt that little sting of jealousy, followed almost immediately by curiosity. Why did I care so much about what a stranger from years ago thought about my wife? That person wasn't in our life anymore. He wasn't part of our relationship. Yet somehow my brain had turned him into a character in a story I couldn't stop thinking about.

Eventually, we talked about my fantasy too. I asked whether she'd ever actually consider acting on it. Her answer was straightforward: she was worried about sexually transmitted infections and didn't feel comfortable taking that risk. The conversation ended without anything dramatic happening between us, but the real drama had already started inside my head.

Afterward, I couldn't stop doing the math. When we met, she was 22. If her estimate was at the higher end, around 20 people including me, that meant she'd already had a significant amount of relationship experience by the time she met me. I was 32 when we met, and my own number was around 15. Ten years older than her, yet she had potentially been with more people than I had.

And suddenly I found myself asking a question I hadn't expected to ask: Is that a lot? The more I thought about it, the less straightforward the answer became. A number by itself doesn't explain someone's life. Twenty people could represent twenty completely different stories, just as fifteen could represent fifteen completely different stories. Some relationships may have lasted months or years. Others may have been brief. Some may have been meaningful. Others may have meant almost nothing. Reducing all of those experiences to a single number suddenly seemed strangely inadequate.

But emotions don't always care about logic. I kept imagining the years before we met. I wondered who she had dated, what those relationships had meant to her, and whether any of those people had left a lasting impression. I wondered whether she'd ever compared me to anyone from her past. I wondered whether she remembered them fondly or barely thought about them anymore. Most of all, I wondered why I was so interested in something that technically had nothing to do with me.

Then I realized something uncomfortable. Maybe I wasn't actually trying to understand her past. Maybe I was trying to understand my place in her present. I wanted reassurance that I was different. I wanted to believe that what we had couldn't be measured against anything that happened before us. I wanted to know that whatever she'd experienced with other people, our relationship was something completely separate.

And the truth is, I already had that answer. She's with me now. She chose this relationship. She built a life with me. Whatever happened before I entered her life is exactly that: before. Still, I couldn't deny that the conversation had changed something in my perspective. Not necessarily something bad, but something I couldn't unthink. I'd asked a question expecting a number, and instead I'd opened a door into a part of my wife's life that I'd never really considered before.

The irony is that my own number is around 15. If a stranger asked me whether 15 was an unusually large number, I'd probably say it depends entirely on the person and their circumstances. Yet when the number belonged to someone I loved, suddenly I was analyzing every detail. Maybe that's what happens when you truly care about someone. Their past feels more important because their present matters so much to you.

I still don't know whether her real number was closer to 15 or 20. Maybe she genuinely doesn't know. Maybe she'll remember something one day and the estimate will change. And honestly, I'm starting to realize that I don't need to know. Because there is one number I actually do know. One. One person she ultimately chose to build a life with. Me. And perhaps that's the number I should have been thinking about all along.
